This repository currently presents the initial project foundation for the 7-Eleven India market re-entry initiative. It outlines the core strategic challenge—evaluating prospective Indian market locations and store operating conditions for a hypothetical re-entry following the 2021–2026 store operations—and establishes the overall scope, stakeholder map, data governance guidelines, and risk management plan. This foundation sets the roadmap, acceptance criteria, and team ownership structure to guide a data-driven market screening and benchmarking study.

Building upon the project foundation, this repository integrates the 7-Eleven India Combined Analytics Dataset (v1.0.0) to drive data preparation, exploratory analysis, and predictive modeling workflows. The underlying architecture processes structured data across transactions (10,000 records spanning 2024), 2,500 customer profiles, 300 candidate store locations across major Indian metros (Mumbai, Pune, Bengaluru, Delhi, Hyderabad, and Chennai), and 720 store-month performance metrics. Engineered features support unsupervised RFM-style customer segmentation, supervised sales forecasting, location screening, and store profitability modeling. Complete data governance practices—including data dictionary standards, strict cleaning logs, outlier handling, DPDP Act 2023 compliance guidelines, and external data source mappings (MoSPI HCES, OpenStreetMap, Kaggle)—are established within this release to ensure complete pipeline auditability.

Our exploratory data analysis evaluates retail performance drivers across 60 stores and 10,000 transactions to inform site selection and operational modeling. Key findings demonstrate that store revenue is strongly driven by daily footfall (Spearman correlation $0.93$) and transit proximity ($0.79$) rather than rent ($0.02$), while high rent burden significantly depresses profit margins. Store profitability varies sharply by location type—mixed-use and high street stores reach over 70% profitability, whereas transit-adjacent stores lag at 35% due to lower footfall and high rents. Furthermore, discounting erodes gross margins from 34% down to 18%–21% without generating any significant lift in sales quantity, and transaction volume peaks around noon and 8 PM without causing significant variations in basket value across hours, channels, or customer membership tiers
